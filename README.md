# Week 2 Journal
So, we still don't know what we're doing. The meeting is soon, and that's okay that we don't know what we're doing yet.

I'm really interested in trying to engage with decolonial principles for this project, since we're working with AbTec, and those being foundational principles that possibly rearrange even the basic assumptions we have about accessing space.

For instance - we typically think of digital space as infinite, and constantly accessible. But how much of this is based in a colonizer mindset? That we can "use" whatever space we have, whenever we want to, in whatever ways we want? These digital spaces are tied to physical resources - water, land - where servers are stored, and how energy is used to power them. We are reaching the end of the digital expense. What does seeking permission to enter a digital space look like, aside from signing away identity/submitting to a surveillance system? Can this be done with respect and trust between the owner of the digital space and the person seeking to access it?

I have a lot of anxiety around changing the existing gallery space into something /I/ think is better - that feels very aligned with the way previous white people have acted in regards to indigenous space. But also asking them permission or having them approve every single little thing would be exhausting and micro-managey. So what's the balance between white guilt(?) and autonomy? 

I'm feeling somewhat lost as to what steps to take going forward, with this group of people I haven't worked with before. I'm also on my last semester and feel like I've already yeeted my brain outside of the university. But I am very excited about this project, and getting to explore interactions within this digital space. It's hard to think of technical restraints or concepts when we don't even know what platform we're going to be creating this in!!

# Questions following the in-class team meeting 16/09/2026

## General questions
- Are we constructing the space for a specific exhibit or are we creating a framework for future exhibits?
- Where and how should we position ourselves to approach this project in a decolonial way? What freedom do we have to offer adjustments to interactions? Are there any guidelines we should go by? 
- Are we creating a virtual gallery that can be visited any time, as it is now in Second Life, or are we creating a VR installation for a physical gallery space? 

## Second Life vs new platform
- How much of the Second Life aesthetics and principles are we looking to keep within this evolution of the gallery space (ie Multiplayer aspects, player avatars, high mobility, etc.)
- What kind of space do we want? Are recreating what already exists on Second Life, or are we looking for a new approach? How are we allowed to change the shape of the space?
- Which limitations are software based, and which are intentional?

## Platform choice
- Are any elements of the gallery more game-like or interactive, such as the shooting range in the current exhibit? Do we need to build specific frameworks to support this content?
- Do we care about company ethics when it comes to the game engine/platform/servers that we are using? What elements are the most important when looking at choice of platform? Ease of creating new exhibit, visual beauty/effects, or platform ethics?
- What controls will be necessary for the artists to create their own exhibit? (lighting, space, walls, what kind of digital elements are included)

## Accessibility
- VR was mentioned in the pitch, but how attached are we to that idea vs at home accessibility? 
- What graphical approach is envisioned? Are we working towards accessibility, therefore as little machine power as possible, or do we want to maximize visual effects such as reflections? 
- If working in VR, which accessibility features do we want to incorporate? (camera rotation, greyscale/colorblind options, popup text, text to speech, etc)
